# Business Overview

Generated by AI-DLC Reverse Engineering — 2026-08-05. Brownfield, two repositories.

## What the combined system does

BIM Group runs a B2B sales operation. Account managers visit client sites, log what happened,
and convert promising visits into deals that progress through quotations and orders. Management
watches pipeline health by sector.

Today that operation is split across **two systems that do not talk to each other**:

| System | Who uses it | Where the data lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bim-crm` (web) | Office staff — sales admin, management, sector heads | MySQL, via Laravel |
| `BIM.visitplan` (mobile) | Field account managers | Cockpit CMS, a separate headless CMS |

A visit logged on a phone lands in Cockpit. A deal raised in the office lands in MySQL. Nothing
reconciles them. The goal of this workflow is to collapse that into one system of record —
`bim-crm` — with the mobile app as a client of it.

## Business transactions

### BT-1 — Field visit lifecycle (mobile, today on Cockpit)

1. Account manager signs in (Entra ID SSO via PKCE, or email+password fallback).
2. A new user lands in a **pending approval** state until an admin approves and assigns a sector.
3. AM plans a visit against a client, with agenda items and a scheduled window.
4. AM **checks in** on site (timestamped), conducts the visit, **checks out**.
5. AM records a **visit outcome** — what happened, next step.
6. Visit rolls up into team reports by AM, sector, and financial quarter.

### BT-2 — Sales pipeline (web, today on MySQL)

1. Source registration or visit plan identifies an opportunity.
2. Opportunity becomes a **deal**, owned by an AM, moving through **per-sector configurable stages**
   (`lead` → `progress` → `won`/`lost`).
3. Deal accumulates attachments, comments, and an observer-driven activity log.
4. Deal produces a **quotation** (line items, discounts, tax, server-side totals, `QT-YYYYMMDD-NN`
   numbering under a row lock).
5. Quotation is shared publicly via token + 6-character password link, or exported as PDF.
6. Accepted quotation becomes a **sale order**; procurement raises **purchase orders** against suppliers.

### BT-3 — Oversight and reporting (web)

Dashboard KPIs, pipeline-by-stage, and five reports — pipeline velocity, quotation win rate,
revenue forecast, sector comparison, visit conversion — each Excel-exportable, gated behind
`reports.view`.

### BT-4 — Access control (web, authoritative)

7 roles, 50 permissions (`spatie/laravel-permission`). Row-level visibility via `visibleTo`/`forUser`
scopes: superadmin and management see everything, sector heads see their sector, everyone else sees
what they own. Navigation renders only what the user may open.

## The gap this workflow closes

`BT-1` runs on Cockpit; `BT-2`, `BT-3`, `BT-4` run on bim-crm. The mobile app therefore cannot:

- show an AM the deals their visits produced,
- respect the CRM's 50-permission RBAC model (Cockpit has its own, weaker, approval model),
- surface quotations, orders, products, suppliers, or any report,
- feed visit data into pipeline reporting without manual re-entry.

Moving the mobile app onto a bim-crm API makes `BT-1` a first-class part of the same pipeline as
`BT-2`, and makes `BT-3`/`BT-4` reusable on mobile rather than reimplemented.

## Constraint discovered during reverse engineering

`bim-crm/ai/PRD.md` records the opposite intent: *"React Native — deferred post-MVP; PWA is primary
mobile delivery"* and *"Laravel Sanctum — session-based SSO only"*. A React Native app nevertheless
exists and is at version 2.6.1 with App Store release notes. **The documented architecture and the
shipped software disagree.** This workflow proceeds on the user's directive (bim-crm as mobile
backend) and treats amending those documents as in-scope work, not as a blocker.
